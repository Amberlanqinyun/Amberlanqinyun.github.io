One source in. A month of publishable content out.

A content engine that turns one source document into a month of platform-native posts, where every line passes a codified voice audit before it ships. Built in Python on Claude agents; the clean-room version is public on GitHub.

By Amber Lan, AI marketing engineer · Last updated 20 August 2026

The pipeline: one source document fans out to platform-native artefacts, but nothing passes the amber gate until it survives the voice audit.

The challenge

One person cannot hand-write platform-native content at the volume a brand needs, and raw model output fails a different way: it reads generated. The tells are recognisable, the voice drifts, and the volume that was supposed to build trust starts eroding it. The problem is not generation. It is quality control at scale.

Constraints

The voice had to be codified, not vibes: rules a machine can check, written down as law. Zero tolerance for recognisable AI patterns. Each platform needed its native shape rather than one post cross-pasted five ways. And the whole pipeline had to run solo, as one stage in a bigger operating week.

The system

Source document in. The engine plans a skeleton per artefact, generates platform-native drafts, then runs every line through a voice gate: a codified audit covering banned words and patterns, sentence rhythm, claim discipline, and the brand's specific rules. Artefacts that fail are rewritten against the specific violations, not regenerated blind. What comes out is a packet: posts shaped for each platform, ready for a human yes or no.

Key decisions

  • The audit is the product. Generation is cheap; the gate is what makes the output shippable. Most of the engineering lives there.
  • Voice as law, not prompt. Style guidance in a prompt decays; codified rules checked line by line do not.
  • Human approves, machine produces. The operator's job collapsed from writing to judging, which is the only part that needed a human.
  • Packets, not posts. One source becomes a coordinated set across platforms, so the calendar fills in one pass.

Output and result

In production use: one source document reliably becomes a month of on-brand, platform-native content, with the operator's time spent on judgment instead of drafting. The engine is packaged as a reusable skill, so pointing it at a new brand means codifying that brand's voice rules rather than rebuilding the pipeline.

Evidence

The clean-room version is public: read the code on GitHub. The distribution half of the same system is also public: Social Media OS.

What I learned

Generated content fails at the last metre, in the specific lines a reader flags as machine-made. Fixing that at the gate, line by line against written law, is what separates a content engine from a content faucet.
