What decides who ranks in local search and AI answers?

Two systems decide, and they read the same record. Google Maps ranks businesses by relevance, distance and prominence, mostly from the Business Profile and reviews. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Copilot and Google's AI features search the web and name the businesses they can confirm across several sources: listings, directories, lists, reviews and the business's own pages.

Google's Business Profile help page says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and that complete and accurate information makes a business more likely to show. OpenAI's help page on ChatGPT search says it rewrites prompts into queries for partner search providers, lists Microsoft among them, and requires sites to allow OAI-SearchBot. So the work is one job with two payoffs: build a single, complete record of the business, then make it appear wherever both systems look.

For New Zealand and Australian businesses, three things differ from the American advice most guides repeat: the government register that anchors the record, the consumer law on reviews and lists, and the directories that rank. This guide covers each, then applies it to Auckland.

Claim the three profiles the engines read

Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Apple Business are the three listings every engine draws on. Google feeds Maps and AI Overviews. Bing feeds Copilot and the search behind ChatGPT. Apple feeds Apple Maps and Siri on every iPhone. All three are free to claim, and Bing can copy the Google profile once it is verified.

Bing's share is small, but the assistants built on it are where many buying questions now start. Bing Places' help page says a listing imported from a verified Google profile is verified automatically and usually publishes in 7 to 12 days, and warns that categories and Google UTM tags may not carry over. Set both by hand after the import.

Give each profile the most specific primary category, your real hours, a description in plain words and a website link tagged with UTM parameters (for example ?utm_source=bing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=places) so your analytics show which listing sent the visit. If you travel to customers, hide the street address and list the areas you serve.

Anchor your details to the NZBN or the ABN record

Every listing should copy one master record: trading name, address or service area, phone and website, written the same way each time. In New Zealand the official version of that record is the NZBN register. In Australia it is your ABN record, shown publicly on ABN Lookup. Bring the register into line first, then copy it everywhere else.

The NZBN register lets a business update its trading name, trading areas, website, email, phone and addresses in MyNZBN. In Australia, the Australian Business Register asks businesses to update their ABN details within 28 days of a change, and requires the main business address to be a physical street address, not a PO box. You can still hide that address on Google if you serve customers at their premises.

The test is simple. Search your old phone number and your old address. Every live result that still shows them is a listing that tells the engines two different stories about one business. Fix those before adding anything new. The glossary entry on NAP consistency sets out the format rules.

Earn reviews the way the law allows

Reviews are the part of prominence you can grow every week, and both countries regulate how. Ask every customer directly with your review link, reply to each review, and never post, buy, filter or reward reviews in ways that mislead. Recent penalties in both countries show regulators treat review manipulation as misleading conduct.

In New Zealand, the Commerce Commission says reviews must be genuine and must not mislead readers about the reviewer's overall message. In August 2026 The TV Shop was ordered to pay $1.104 million for conduct that included staff reviews posted without disclosure and low ratings held back.

In Australia, the ACCC says incentives for reviews must apply whether the review is positive or negative and must be clearly disclosed, and that suppressing or editing negative reviews may mislead. The same page records the Federal Court ordering HealthEngine to pay $2.9 million, including for publishing misleading patient reviews.

A steady rhythm beats a burst. Ask on the day the work finishes, while the result is fresh, and keep the request the same for every customer.

Get onto the lists and directories AI answers cite

Assistants answer "who is the best..." questions mostly from other people's pages: directories, review platforms, industry member lists and articles that rank businesses in a city. Search your own service and city, note the directories and lists on page one, and get listed there with the master record. They are your market's citation set.

Write pages an answer engine can quote

Your own site confirms what the listings say. Give each core service a page, and each city you really serve a page with facts specific to it. Open each with a 40 to 80 word answer naming the business, the service, the place and who it is for. Add matching schema, allow the AI crawlers, and publish an llms.txt.

Answer engines lift short, self-contained passages. A first paragraph such as "Smith Electrical is a licensed electrician in Takapuna serving homes and small businesses across the North Shore, with same-day call-outs" is easy to quote. A paragraph that opens with a mission statement is not. Put the city and service in the page title and main heading, and price ranges in local currency where you can.

Mark the business up as the most specific schema.org type that fits (ProfessionalService, Plumber, AccountingService and so on) with the same name, phone and locality as the profiles, areaServed listing the places you cover, and sameAs links to each profile. Check robots.txt allows Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, and add an llms.txt file with a one-line description of the business. The AEO checklist covers the rest.

Ranking higher in Auckland

Auckland rewards precision. "Near me" means very different things in Albany and Ōtāhuhu, so set your Business Profile's service areas to the suburbs and areas you really serve, write one strong page per part of the city where most of your work comes from, and join the local lists Aucklanders and assistants trust.

Group your pages the way Aucklanders talk: the North Shore, West, East and South Auckland, the central isthmus, Rodney and Franklin. Google allows up to 20 service areas, within about two hours' drive of your base, so a central city business might list Ponsonby, Grey Lynn, Parnell and Newmarket rather than the whole region. Then write prompts the way an Aucklander would, such as "good accountant for a café in Mt Eden", and check who is named.

Auckland also has the clearest warning on lists. On 5 October 2026 the Commerce Commission took Top Reviews and Auckland Magazine to the Auckland High Court, alleging they sold places on "The 10 Best" lists while presenting them as independent reviews by local staff writers. If a list approaches you for a fee, ask whether the payment will be disclosed.

The better Auckland mentions come from real relationships: the Auckland Business Chamber, whose entry membership includes an online directory listing, town centre business associations, local news and the clubs and schools you support.
