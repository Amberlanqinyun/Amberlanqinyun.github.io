How do ChatGPT and Google Maps choose which Auckland businesses to recommend?

Both look for a business that matches the request and sits close to the person asking, then favour the ones with a good reputation. Google Maps reads that mostly from your Business Profile. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode search the web, map listings and reviews, then name the businesses whose details they can find and confirm across several sources.

Local AI search is the way assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode answer place-based questions by combining web pages, map listings and reviews into a short list of named businesses. A person types "good family lawyer in Henderson" and gets four or five names with a sentence on each, often without seeing a results page at all.

The work that wins those answers overlaps almost entirely with good local SEO. This guide applies it to Auckland: a city of distinct suburbs and town centres where "near me" means very different things in Howick and in Kumeū. For the general version, start with the guide to getting your business mentioned by ChatGPT.

How does Google decide who appears in Maps?

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and how well known a business is. Relevance is how closely your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence comes from reviews, ratings and links to your business across the web. You can influence two of the three.

Google's Business Profile help page on local ranking says results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and explains the third factor under the heading prominence: how many websites link to the business, how many reviews it has, and its ratings. The same page says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, and that businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show in local results.

Distance is fixed for any one search. A plumber based in Ōtāhuhu will rarely top a search made in Albany, however good the profile. What you control is relevance, which comes from a category and services list that match the words people use, and prominence, which grows with reviews and with mentions on other Auckland sites. Spend your effort there.

Set up your Google Business Profile for Auckland

A strong Auckland profile has the most specific primary category for your main work, the suburbs you serve listed as service areas, accurate hours including public holidays, real photos, a services list in plain words, and a steady flow of recent reviews with replies. Posting short updates each week keeps the profile current and gives customers a reason to call.

Categories

Pick the single primary category that describes the job you most want more of, such as "Electrician" rather than "Contractor". Search Google's category list for the most specific match, then add secondary categories for the rest of your work. The category does more for relevance than anything you write in the description.

Service areas: list the suburbs you serve

If you travel to customers, hide your street address and set service areas. Google's service area guidance allows up to 20 service areas by city, postcode or other area, asks that the whole area stay within about two hours' drive of your base, and says edits can take up to 48 hours to show. In Auckland, list the suburbs and areas you really work in, for example Ponsonby, Grey Lynn, Herne Bay and Westmere for a central city electrician. A tight, honest list matches the way people search better than one entry for the whole region.

Reviews

Ask every customer, the day the job finishes, with your direct review link. Recency matters: BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of 1,002 US adults found 74% look for reviews written in the last three months. BrightLocal sells local SEO software and the sample is American, so treat it as a direction for New Zealand. Reply to each review with a detail from the job. Never offer discounts for reviews or ask only the customers you expect to be happy, since both break Google's review policy.

Posts and photos

Google lets you publish short updates on your profile: a finished job in Mt Eden, a new service, holiday hours for Matariki or Auckland Anniversary Day. Add two or three real photos a month. Their value lies in showing people (and the assistants reading your profile) that the business is active.

Why do consistent details and your NZBN matter?

Assistants recommend businesses they can confirm. If your website, Business Profile, Facebook page and directory listings show different phone numbers, old addresses or two trading names, each source weakens the others. Keeping name, address and phone identical everywhere, including the official NZBN register, gives every system one version of the facts to repeat.

This is NAP consistency: the same name, address and phone number, written the same way, on every listing. Pick one format for your address, with unit, street, suburb and postcode in a fixed order, and copy it exactly. If you moved from Newmarket to Penrose three years ago, search for the old address and update every listing that still shows it.

The NZBN register lets a business update its primary business data in MyNZBN, including trading name, trading areas, website, email, phone and contact addresses. It is a government record that other services draw on. Nothing published shows ChatGPT reading it directly, but it costs ten minutes to bring into line, and it removes one more conflicting record.

Should you build pages for Auckland suburbs?

Build a page for each service you sell, and one for each part of Auckland where you do enough work to describe it in real detail. A page on "Bathroom renovations on the North Shore" that names jobs, travel times and typical prices gives Google and assistants something specific to match. Suburb pages with only the name swapped get ignored.

Group suburbs the way Aucklanders talk about them: North Shore, West Auckland, East Auckland, South Auckland, the central isthmus, Rodney and Franklin. One strong page per area you serve, with the suburbs listed inside it, usually beats forty thin ones. Each page should open with a direct answer (what you do, where, for whom), then cover price ranges in NZD, how quickly you can get there, and examples of local work.

Put the area in the page title and main heading, link each area page from your service pages, and point your Business Profile's website link at the most relevant page. The SEO guide for New Zealand small business covers the page structure and technical checks in more depth.

Where do ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode get local answers?

They search. ChatGPT sends rewritten queries to partner search providers and can use local business listings from third parties. Perplexity searches the web live and cites numbered sources. Google AI Mode runs several related searches across data sources, and Google asks owners to keep Business Profile details current. All of them read web and maps data you can influence.

OpenAI's help page on searching the web with ChatGPT says it rewrites prompts into targeted queries for partner search providers, uses an approximate location from your IP address so "near me" becomes a search for your city, may share location with third-party providers of nearby business listings, and lists Microsoft among the providers. The same page says that to be eligible, a website must allow OAI-SearchBot to crawl it. Check your robots.txt and your hosting firewall.

Perplexity's help centre says it searches the internet in real time and gives each answer numbered citations to the original sources. Google Search Central's page on AI features describes AI Mode issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources, says there are no extra requirements to appear in it, and advises owners to keep Merchant Center and Business Profile information up to date.

Because Bing results feed ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot, claim a Bing Places listing as well. Statcounter's September 2026 data gives Bing 7.71% of New Zealand search, against Google's 89.02%, so its value here comes mostly from the assistants built on it.

People act on these answers. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of US consumers used ChatGPT or other AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% a year earlier, and its follow-up on the 455 AI users in that sample found 88% check the sources the AI cites and 97% sometimes cross-check AI picks against reviews on the original platforms. Closer to home, InternetNZ's research with Verian, surveying 1,003 New Zealand adults in late 2025, found 55% had used AI in search engines in the past year.

Which Auckland directories and listings are worth claiming?

Claim the listings that customers in your trade really use and that assistants are likely to find: Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Facebook, the national directories, your trade's own platforms and any Auckland membership directory you qualify for. Mentions on local sites help as well, because they show other people vouching for you.

  • The national directories Yellow and Finda, plus Neighbourly, which organises by suburb. Fill each one in with the same NAP format.
  • Trade platforms such as Builders Crack and NoCowboys, and your industry body's "find a member" search.
  • The Auckland Business Chamber's entry membership is listed at $180 plus GST a year and includes an online directory listing. Join if the events and support suit you; the listing is a side benefit.
  • Town centre and community sites. Many Auckland town centres have a business association with a member list, and local news sites, school newsletters and sports clubs mention sponsors and suppliers.

Mentions matter for AI answers in particular. Ahrefs' own study of 75,000 brands found branded web mentions correlated with AI Overview visibility at 0.664, against 0.218 for backlinks. It is a correlation from a company that sells SEO tools, so read it as a pointer: being talked about on other sites helps you get named.

Thirty days is enough to put the foundations in place. Spend week one on the Business Profile, week two on consistent details and the NZBN, week three on service and area pages, and week four on directories, crawler access and a baseline. How do you check whether it is working?

Track two kinds of signal each month. In Google, watch Business Profile calls, website clicks and direction requests, plus Search Console clicks for local searches. In AI assistants, run the same five local prompts and record who is named and which sources are cited. Enquiries are the number that matters, so ask new customers how they found you.

Write prompts the way an Auckland customer would: "who is a good physio in Mt Albert", "emergency electrician North Shore tonight", "best Korean restaurant for a group in Northcote". Run them in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode and Copilot from a fresh session, and include the suburb, since OpenAI's own advice for better local results is to include your city, neighbourhood or postcode. When a competitor is named and you are not, open the sources cited. They show you which listing or article to work on next.
