What is an AI marketing agency?

An AI marketing agency is a marketing provider whose delivery runs on AI systems it builds and operates, with people setting direction and reviewing the output. In New Zealand the label is used broadly, so two agencies with the same title can sell very different things, and the useful question is what the AI does for you.

An AI marketing agency is a marketing provider whose delivery runs on AI systems it builds and operates, such as agents, automations and models, with people setting direction and reviewing the output before it reaches customers.

The demand behind the label is real. LocaliQ's December 2025 trends report, from 730+ small business owners and marketers (74% in the US or Canada), found nearly 60% using AI for some marketing and 60% working with at least one marketing partner. Owners already use AI themselves and already buy help, so agencies have added AI to the name. What sits behind the name varies.

What does an AI marketing agency do in practice?

In practice an AI marketing agency in NZ falls into one of five types: a traditional agency using AI tools internally, an AI tool installer, a productised AI-led package, an AI search or AEO specialist, or a setup-and-train consultancy. Each type suits a different business, and some agencies combine two.

Most owners asking "what does an AI marketing agency do" have the third or fourth type in mind: marketing that runs on a system and costs less than people-hours. The first type is the most common in practice, and it can be a perfectly good agency. It is simply a different purchase.

How can you tell real AI systems from an AI label?

You can tell real AI systems from a label by asking to see a system run, checking whether the price and speed reflect it, and confirming what you keep if you leave. An agency running real systems can show inputs, outputs and the review step in a few minutes. A label usually leads to a capability deck.

Five checks that take one meeting:

  1. Show me it running. Ask for a live or recorded run on a real business: what it read, what it produced, who approved it. Screenshots of a tool's home screen are not a system.
  2. Show me the pricing logic. If the work runs on systems, the price usually reflects output or scope. A retainer billed in hours at the usual agency rate suggests the AI saves the agency time, which is fine, but the saving stays with the agency.
  3. Show me where the human sits. Someone should approve anything published under your name, any customer contact and any spend. If nobody reviews, that is a risk. If everything waits for a meeting, the system is slower than it looks.
  4. Show me what I keep. Pages, schema, tracking, content and automations should live on your accounts. Anything that disappears when you cancel was rented.
  5. Show me the report. Enquiries, their sources, cost per enquiry and what to stop. Follower counts and impressions alone tell you little.

The same checks apply to any provider selling an AI marketing agent. The guide to AI marketing agents sets out what a complete one covers.

What should an AI SEO or AEO agency deliver?

An AI SEO or AEO agency should deliver changes that help AI answers quote your business: clear question-and-answer pages, schema markup, consistent business details across the web, and a report on which AI answers now mention you. The work sits on your own site and stays there when the engagement ends.

Answer engine optimisation is a young field, and the research behind it is still early. Aggarwal and colleagues' KDD 2024 paper on generative engine optimisation found content changes could lift visibility in generative engine answers by up to 40%, tested on a research benchmark. That is a reason to take the work seriously, and also a reason to be wary of any agency promising a guaranteed AI ranking.

When comparing the "best AEO agency" candidates, look for four things: their own business appears in AI answers for their own category, they explain every change in plain language, they test a fixed set of customer questions before and after, and they start with a fixed-scope project. What should you ask before you sign?

Before you sign, ask what the agency's systems read, what they produce each month, who reviews the work, what you keep, how results are reported and how you leave. Clear written answers to those questions protect you better than any contract clause, and a good agency will welcome them.

Value is where client relationships tend to break. Setup's 2025 Marketing Relationship Survey found 61% of clients who ended an agency relationship cited dissatisfaction with value, up from 36% in 2024, though from only about 100 responses, mostly larger US brands. Questions that test value early:

  • What will exist at the end of month one that does not exist today?
  • Which of our numbers will you report, and how often?
  • Which tasks are done by your systems, and which by people?
  • Who approves claims, prices and guarantees before they go out?
  • Which accounts and files are in our name from day one?
  • What is the minimum term and the notice period?
  • What would make you tell us to stop spending on a channel?

When does a simpler setup serve you better?

A simpler setup serves you better when your work mostly comes from referrals, you only need one channel, you or a staff member have a few hours a week to run it, or your marketing budget is below about NZD 1,000 a month. In those cases AI tools, one freelancer or a written plan go further.

Four common cases:

  • Referral-led businesses. LocaliQ found 65% of small businesses name customer referrals as their top source of new customers. A review request process and a strong Google Business Profile may do more than any agency.
  • One channel. If one channel brings the work, a specialist freelancer for that channel usually costs less than a multi-channel agency.
  • Owner time available. An owner with three or four hours a week and general AI tools can cover the basics well, especially with a written plan to follow.
  • Small budget. King Tide's guide advises against hiring anyone below NZD 1,000 to 1,500 a month. Below that, spend on the website and tracking first.
