What do Claude and ChatGPT business plans cost in 2026?

Claude's Team plan and ChatGPT Business have the same list price. A Standard seat costs US$20 per user per month billed annually, or US$25 billed monthly, and both plans need at least two seats. Each also sells a Premium seat with about five times the usage at US$100 annually or US$125 monthly.

The figures come from each vendor's own pages, checked on 16 October 2026. Anthropic's help centre lists Team Standard seats at $20 per member per month billed annually and $25 billed monthly, says these are prices for US customers excluding tax, and sets the plan at two to 150 seats. OpenAI's help centre lists ChatGPT Business Standard seats at US$20 billed annually and US$25 billed monthly, with a two seat minimum, and notes that ChatGPT Team was renamed ChatGPT Business on 29 August 2025.

New Zealand visitors see local figures. OpenAI's pricing page, viewed from New Zealand, showed ChatGPT Business at NZ$33 per user per month billed annually and NZ$41 billed monthly, without saying whether GST is included. Anthropic's pricing page, viewed from New Zealand, showed Team Standard at $40 a seat billed annually and $50 monthly, with the note "Prices include 15% GST" and no currency code. Exchange rates move, so the NZD price of a USD plan moves with them.

What does a business plan give you that a personal plan does not?

A business AI plan is a paid workspace from an AI vendor that gives each staff member a seat under one company account, with central billing, admin controls and business data terms that exclude your content from model training by default. Personal plans bill one person, and the training setting is the user's to manage.

That last point matters most for a business. On Anthropic's personal Pro and Max plans, the pricing page lists model training as opt-out. On the Team plan it lists "None by default". OpenAI's comparison table makes the same split: content on individual plans can be used for training with an opt-out available, while Business content is not used. A business plan also gives you an owner and admins who add and remove people from one company account, which keeps access tidy when staff change.

For a sole trader, a personal plan with training switched off can be enough. Once two or more people share client information, the business plan is the safer default.

Should you pay monthly or annually?

Pay monthly for the first month or two, while you learn who uses the tool every week, then move to annual billing for the seats you are sure of. Annual billing cuts the Standard seat price from US$25 to US$20 on both plans, a 20% saving, but you commit the seat count for the year.

The rules on changing seats differ. OpenAI's billing article says seats added on an annual ChatGPT Business plan stay committed through the end of the term, reductions take effect at the next billing cycle, and you are charged for every seat whether or not it is assigned. Anthropic's pricing table lists adding seats mid-term on Team, with card payment only. Neither self-serve plan offers invoicing; that sits with the Enterprise tiers.

A sensible pattern for a 10 person business: buy monthly seats for the four or five people who will use the tool daily, run a four to six week trial, then add seats and switch to annual once usage settles.

Is your data used for training, and what does the NZ Privacy Act expect?

Both vendors say business plan content is excluded from model training by default. That covers the vendor's side. Under the Privacy Act 2020, your business remains responsible for any personal information staff put into either tool, so you still need rules on what goes in and who can use it.

On the vendor side, OpenAI's business data page says it does not use data from ChatGPT Business or Enterprise, including inputs or outputs, for training by default. Anthropic's privacy centre says it will not use inputs or outputs from its commercial products for training by default, that feedback such as a thumbs rating can be used, and that Team and Enterprise owners can turn rating off. If you want nothing used, switch that setting off on day one.

On your side, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner says the Privacy Act applies to everyone using AI tools in New Zealand, recommends a privacy impact assessment before you start, and advises that if in doubt you do not use AI tools to handle personal information. The same office says that in most circumstances you remain responsible for personal information you put in the cloud. Customers notice, too: the Commissioner's 2026 privacy survey of 1,245 New Zealanders, run in March and April 2026, found 82% want more say in how their information is collected and used.

A short policy covers most of it: which client data may go into the tool, which may not, who reviews AI output before it reaches a customer, and who owns the workspace settings. Why do setup and adoption cost more than the seats?

For a 10 person team, US$200 to US$250 a month is small next to the hours it takes to make the tool useful. Someone has to set up the workspace, connect the right tools, write the shared instructions and templates, train staff and check the output. Those hours decide whether the seats pay back.

Skills are the gap. KPMG and the University of Melbourne's 2025 global study, surveyed over summer 2024-25, found only 24% of New Zealanders have formal or informal training in AI, and 51% of New Zealand employees have relied on AI output at work without evaluating its accuracy. A paid seat in untrained hands produces generic work and unchecked mistakes.

A first month that tends to work:

  1. Pick one plan, buy monthly seats for the daily users, and switch off feedback rating if you want no content used for training.
  2. Write a one-page brief the whole team shares: your business, your customers, your tone, and what never goes into the tool.
  3. Turn two or three repeated jobs into saved instructions or templates, such as a proposal first draft or a weekly report.
  4. Connect one work tool, read-only first, and check what the assistant can see.
  5. Agree a human in the loop rule: a named person reviews anything that reaches a customer.
  6. After four to six weeks, count who uses it weekly, then set the seat count and move to annual billing.
