SEO for small business in New Zealand: a practical guide.

The short answer

SEO for a New Zealand small business starts with Google, which handles about nine in ten NZ searches. Most SEO advice in New Zealand is written by agencies selling SEO. This guide puts the work in the order a small business should do it, and marks which parts you can do yourself.

01What does SEO mean for a small business in New Zealand?

For a New Zealand small business, SEO means being the business Google shows when someone nearby searches for what you sell. That comes down to a complete Google Business Profile, recent reviews, a clear page for each service and area, and a website Google can crawl. Most of it is free, and most of it is ordinary work.

Search engine optimisation (SEO) is the work of making a business's website and listings easy for search engines to find, understand and show to people searching for what the business sells. For a 5 to 50 person business, local search is where the enquiries are: the plumber in Hamilton or the physio in Wellington who gets the call because they appeared in the map results.

This guide is in the order I would do the work. If you are new to AI in your marketing generally, the start-here guide to AI marketing for NZ small business sets the wider context.

02Where do New Zealand customers search?

New Zealand customers search on Google, overwhelmingly. Google held 89.02% of NZ search engine market share in September 2026, with Bing a distant second. AI assistants are growing fast as a research tool, but they mostly sit alongside search, and several of them read search results to build their answers.

Statcounter's September 2026 figures give Google 89.02% of New Zealand search and Bing 7.71%1, and its Australian figures give Google 87.9% and Bing 9.17%2. A small survey points the same way: IAB New Zealand's Kiwi Search Habits 2025, from 250 upper North Island adults surveyed in March 2025, found 96.8% named Google as their main search engine and 69.2% go to a search engine often or always before a purchase3.

AI is changing how people use that search. InternetNZ's research with Verian, surveying 1,003 NZ adults in late 2025, found 55% had used AI in search engines in the past year4. Among the shoppers who already use AI, PwC New Zealand's July 2026 survey of 1,000 NZ consumers found 44% said AI had reduced their use of search engines when researching purchases5. That sample covers AI users only, so it is a direction of travel. Google remains the place to start.

03Set up and maintain your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is the free listing that controls how you appear in Google Maps and the local results. Claim and verify it, choose the most accurate primary category, set your hours and service areas, and add photos and services. Then keep it current, because an out-of-date profile costs you enquiries without telling you.

The settings that make the most difference:

  1. Primary category. Pick the single category that best matches the work you most want, such as "Electrician" rather than "Contractor". Add secondary categories for the rest.
  2. Business name. Use your real trading name. Adding keywords or suburbs to the name breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended.
  3. Address or service area. If customers come to you, show the address. If you go to them, set service areas by town or suburb and hide the street address.
  4. Hours. Include special hours for public holidays such as Waitangi Day, Anzac Day and Matariki. Wrong holiday hours generate bad reviews.
  5. Services and description. List each service in plain words customers use, and write a description that says what you do and where.
  6. Photos. Real photos of your team, vehicles, premises and finished work. Add a few each month.
  7. Website link. Point it to the most relevant page, which for a single-location business is usually the home page.

Set a reminder for the first working day of each month to check hours, add photos and post an update. Do the same for a Bing Places listing, which takes ten minutes and feeds Microsoft's search and Copilot.

04How do you get more Google reviews?

You get more reviews by asking every satisfied customer, at the moment the job is done, with a direct link that opens the review form. A steady flow matters as much as the total, because customers look at how recent reviews are. Reply to every review, positive or negative, within a few days, in a calm and specific voice.

The best review data available is American, so read it as direction for New Zealand. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of 1,002 US adults found 97% read reviews for local businesses, 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, and 74% look for reviews from the last three months6. BrightLocal sells local SEO software, which is worth knowing, but the pattern matches what most owners see on their own profiles.

A review routine that fits a busy week:

  • Copy the review link from your Google Business Profile and shorten it.
  • Send it by text message the same day the job finishes, with the customer's name and the job in the message.
  • Ask everyone. Asking only the customers you expect to be happy breaks Google's review policy, and so does offering discounts or gifts for reviews.
  • Reply to each review with a detail from the job, which shows future customers a real person runs the business.
  • Answer negative reviews once, factually, with an offer to sort it out offline.

Never post or buy fake reviews. They breach Google's policies, and misleading customers about your reputation can also breach the Fair Trading Act.

05Build one page per service and one per location

Each service you want to be found for needs its own page, and so does each town or area you actively serve. A single "Services" page listing ten things gives Google nothing specific to rank. A page called "Heat pump installation in Hamilton" that explains the job, the price range and the area gives it a clear match.

A good service page answers the questions a customer asks on the phone. Put the answer first: what the service is, where you do it and who it suits. Then cover typical price ranges in NZD, how long it takes, what is included, the brands or standards you work to, and your licences or registrations. Close with a clear way to get in touch.

Location pages need real local content to be useful. Write about the suburbs you cover, jobs you have done there, local conditions that affect the work, and how quickly you can get there. Copying one page and swapping the town name produces thin pages that Google tends to ignore. If you only serve one area, a strong home page and service pages are enough.

Each page needs a unique title tag that starts with the service and place, a one-line meta description that a person would click, one main heading, and links to related pages on your site.

06Fix the technical basics

The technical basics are the checks that let Google find, read and show your pages: Search Console set up and verified, a sitemap submitted, important pages indexed, a site that works on a phone, and pages that load quickly over HTTPS. A non-technical owner can complete these in an afternoon using free tools from Google and Microsoft.

  1. Verify your domain in Google Search Console and submit your sitemap. Most website builders create one at /sitemap.xml.
  2. Use the Pages report to see which pages are indexed and why others are not. Fix anything important that is excluded.
  3. Run your main pages through Google's PageSpeed Insights and fix the largest problems, which are usually oversized images.
  4. Open your site on your own phone and try to call you, find your hours and fill in your contact form.
  5. Check every page loads on https and that the old http address redirects.
  6. Import your Search Console site into Bing Webmaster Tools so Bing, and the assistants that use it, can crawl the same pages.
  7. Look at your robots.txt file and make sure it does not block search engines or AI search crawlers you want to reach you.

Add LocalBusiness structured data to your contact or home page if your website builder supports it. It keeps your name, address and hours machine readable. It will not rank you on its own.

07How does SEO help you show up in AI search?

SEO is the foundation for AI search. Google states that its AI Overviews and AI Mode need nothing beyond core SEO, and ChatGPT uses partner search engines to find pages. Clear service pages, a complete Business Profile and consistent details are what assistants read. Reviews and mentions on other sites then add weight to the answer.

Google Search Central says there is no special schema, AI text file or markup needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode7. Those summaries now show on buying searches as well as questions. Semrush's study of more than 10 million US desktop keywords found commercial, transactional and navigational queries made up 42.8% of AI Overview triggers by October 20258.

That changes how people click. Pew Research Center's March 2025 study of 900 US adults found users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% when it did not9. For a local business, that makes it more valuable to be named inside the summary and to have a Business Profile people can act on directly. 08What can you do yourself, and when should you hire help?

Most owners can handle the Business Profile, reviews and Search Console themselves, because these are routine tasks with free tools. Service page writing is manageable with a few hours a week. Hire help when the technical fixes go beyond your website builder, when you compete in a crowded Auckland market, or when the weekly hours simply are not there.

If you hire, compare a few options on price and scope. Clickthrough's published SEO packages start at NZD 960 plus GST a month for a small business10, The SEO Guy lists retainers from NZD 795 plus GST a month with a six-month minimum11, and OnlineNZ's programmes start at NZD 499 a month excluding GST12. Across those three price pages, published NZ SEO packages run from about NZD 500 to 2,500 a month plus GST. The guide to marketing agency costs in NZ compares those figures with freelancers and in-house hires.

Before you sign anything, ask what will be done each month, who owns the pages and accounts, and how results will be reported. Enquiries and calls are the numbers that matter; ranking reports alone are not enough.

09A first-month SEO plan for a small business

A realistic first month takes three to five hours a week. Week one covers accounts and technical checks, week two the Business Profile and review routine, week three your most important service pages, and week four location pages, directories and a baseline. After that, the work becomes about an hour a week.

  1. Week 1: accounts and checks. Verify Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the sitemap, review the Pages report, run PageSpeed Insights on the home page and your top service page, and test the site on your phone.
  2. Week 2: Business Profile and reviews. Complete every section of the Google Business Profile, set holiday hours, add ten real photos, claim Bing Places, and send review requests to every customer from the past month.
  3. Week 3: service pages. Write or rewrite pages for your two or three most profitable services, answer-first, with price ranges, areas and a clear contact option.
  4. Week 4: locations, listings and a baseline. Add a location page for each area you actively serve, make your name, address and phone number identical across NZ directories, and record a baseline: Search Console clicks, Business Profile calls and enquiries, plus what ChatGPT and Google AI Mode say when asked for your service in your town.

From month two, add one useful page a month, keep the review routine going and compare the numbers against your baseline each month. If you want an outside read on where to start, the free AI-readiness benchmark takes a few minutes and needs no email.

12Questions owners ask

Can I do SEO for my small business myself?

Yes. The work that matters most for a local NZ business is free and needs no technical skill: completing your Google Business Profile, asking for reviews, writing one clear page per service and per area, and checking Google Search Console once a month. Budget three to five hours a week for the first month, then about an hour a week to keep it going.

How long does SEO take to work in New Zealand?

Changes to your Google Business Profile and fixes to indexing problems can show up within a few weeks. New service and location pages usually take two to four months to settle in the results, longer in competitive trades in Auckland. Track enquiries and Search Console clicks monthly and judge the trend over a quarter, because week-to-week movement is mostly noise.

How much does SEO cost for a small business in NZ?

Doing it yourself costs your time and nothing else, since Google Business Profile and Search Console are free. Published NZ agency packages from Clickthrough, The SEO Guy and OnlineNZ run from about NZD 500 to 2,500 a month plus GST. The marketing agency cost guide on this site breaks those prices down alongside freelancer and in-house options.

Do I still need SEO now that people use ChatGPT?

Yes. Google still handles about 89% of New Zealand searches according to Statcounter's September 2026 data, and AI assistants lean on search results and search partners to find businesses. Google says its AI features need nothing beyond core SEO. Good SEO is the foundation that AI search builds on, with reviews and mentions on other sites adding weight.

Do I need a .co.nz domain for SEO in New Zealand?

A .co.nz domain helps Google and customers see that you serve New Zealand, and it is the usual choice for a local business. A .com can still rank here if the site clearly states its NZ location, uses NZD prices and has a Google Business Profile. If you already have a .com that works, moving domains is rarely worth the disruption.

What is the most important SEO task for a local business?

For most local NZ businesses, it is the Google Business Profile: the right primary category, accurate hours including public holidays, service areas, photos and a steady flow of reviews with replies. It controls how you appear in Google Maps and the local results, and it is free. Service pages on your own site come next.
