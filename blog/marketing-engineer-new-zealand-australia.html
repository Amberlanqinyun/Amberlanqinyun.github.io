What is a marketing engineer? A role guide for New Zealand and Australia.

The short answer

A marketing engineer builds the AI systems that produce a company's marketing, runs them every week on the company's own accounts, and keeps the judgement over what reaches a customer. In New Zealand and Australia the title is still rare in job ads, while the work is already advertised under marketing technology and lifecycle titles.

I run a whole marketing function as a marketing engineer, in Auckland, on agents I built. This guide describes the role as it is done, so you can judge whether your business needs one, and whether the person in front of you is one.

01The role in one sentence

A marketing engineer is a marketer who builds the AI systems that produce a company's marketing, runs them on the company's own accounts, and keeps the judgement over what reaches a customer. The first half is engineering. The second half is the marketing taste that decides whether the output is any good.

That is my working definition. I have done this job since April 2026, running a whole marketing function solo at an Auckland company on agents I built, and the definition is written from the inside. It is also a young title. No statistics agency in New Zealand or Australia counts it yet, and I am going to resist inventing a market to make it look inevitable. The evidence is the work: more marketing production is handed to AI systems every quarter, and someone has to be accountable for how those systems behave in front of customers.

Two neighbours are worth separating before going further. A GTM engineer builds the systems that feed sales, such as prospect lists, enrichment and sequencing. A marketing automation specialist wires up the tools a marketing team already uses. The marketing engineer's object is heavier than either: the system that plans, produces, checks and reports the marketing itself. The full definition and the GTM comparison sit in their own guide. This one is about the role as it is done here, in New Zealand and Australia.

02What a marketing engineer actually does

Five things make up the work. Take any one away and you have a different job: an analyst, an automation contractor, or a marketer with a new title. The five are writing the positioning into the system, building the agents on the company's accounts, reviewing every output, measuring with numbers that carry their source, and documenting the system so the team keeps it.

  1. Writing the positioning into the system. Who the company sells to, what it claims and what it can prove live in one brief that every agent reads before it writes a word. When the brief is wrong the agents are wrong in the same way fifty times, so this is the piece I spend the most time getting right with a founder.
  2. Building the agents on the company's own accounts. A search agent that fixes how the business appears in Google and in AI answers. A content engine that turns one idea into a month of drafts. An outbound agent that researches a hundred buyers and stops the moment one replies. A reporting agent that reads the analytics and writes one page. Each lives in the business's own tools, so nothing walks out the door with the engineer.
  3. Reviewing every output. Agents produce drafts. A named person decides what ships. In my own function that person is me, and the review takes more of the week than the building does. KPMG and the University of Melbourne found 51% of New Zealand employees had relied on AI output at work without evaluating its accuracy1 (surveyed over summer 2024-25). The human in the loop is the part of this role a business is paying for.
  4. Measuring with numbers that carry their source. Every figure in a report says where it came from: the analytics property, the ad platform, the CRM, or a model, with its date range. A number without a basis is treated as a rumour, and the agent that produced it is corrected.
  5. Documenting the system so the team keeps it. The instructions, the review rules and the prompts live in files the team can read, change and hand to the next person. When a run fails, the fix goes into the files, so the system improves instead of one person's memory improving.

None of this guarantees a result. A well-built system still depends on a human deciding what the business should do next. What it does guarantee is that the deciding happens on evidence you can inspect.

03Marketing engineer vs the roles New Zealand and Australian businesses already hire

Most local businesses already employ or contract one of five roles for their marketing. The marketing engineer overlaps with each and replaces none of them outright. The difference is the object of the work: the others direct people or tools, while the engineer builds and runs the system that does the producing, then stands behind what it sends.

04The system behind the role

The marketing engineer's central artefact is a system, and a good one has four layers: the context the agents reason from, the bounded jobs they may do, the checks that catch what they got wrong, and the review where a person decides. A stronger model helps. Only a stronger system makes the work inspectable and safe to hand to someone else.

In my function the context layer is a positioning brief and a set of written voice rules. The jobs are ten agents, one per marketing job, from AI search to reporting. The checks are small and dull: a banned-word scan, a claims check against a register of what the company can prove, a link check, a source check on every number. The review is mine, on everything that reaches a customer. Running marketing on Claude Code is the working-notes version of how that is set up, and how a build runs shows the same system as a client would meet it.

What I leave out of public writing is the operational detail: the exact thresholds, the gate checklists, the client-specific rules. Those encode judgement that belongs to the business that paid for it. The method publishes. The recipes stay with the client.

05Skills and tools, in order of weight

Three capabilities carry the role, and the tool list matters less than the order. First, marketing judgement: knowing what good output looks like, and when a system should send nothing at all. Second, verification design: turning "looks right" into a check that can fail. Third, enough building skill to make and fix agents in production.

  • Marketing judgement. Positioning, editorial standards, what a claim needs behind it. This is the half most engineering candidates lack, and it is the half that keeps a business out of trouble. Salesforce's State of Marketing, surveying 4,450 marketers in late 2025, found only 13% using agentic AI2, so most teams are still deciding what good looks like.
  • Verification design. A schema check, a character limit, a prohibited-claim scan, a read-back that compares what shipped with what was approved. This is the skill that separates engineering from enthusiasm, and the one I would test for first in an interview.
  • Building in an agentic environment. Comfort in a command-line tool such as Claude Code, where context, permissions and checks are plain files that can be versioned and reviewed. Comfort with APIs and a scripting language. Comfort inside the marketing tools themselves: GA4, Search Console, HubSpot or whatever CRM the business runs, the ad platforms, LinkedIn.

Businesses hiring for the build half of this role often search for an Claude Code consultant or an AI agent developer before they search for a marketing engineer. Both are fair descriptions of the work, and both pages describe how a build with me runs.

06What the role pays in New Zealand and Australia

There is no published salary band for the marketing engineer title in either country, so the honest answer is a range built from the roles either side of it. On SEEK New Zealand in October 2026, a marketing manager was advertised at NZ$105,000 to NZ$125,000. Recruiter estimates for senior AI roles sit near NZ$195,000. A marketing engineer lands between those, depending on how much of the role is building.

  • Talent International's March 2026 list of the highest-paying technology jobs in New Zealand gives indicative estimates of around NZ$195,000 for senior AI roles such as agent architect4, and labels them estimates.
  • For a sense of where the ceiling sits overseas, Figma's Marketing Engineer, AI Deployment ad lists a US$127,000 to US$296,000 base5. Local pay will not match that, and local cost of living and spend levels explain most of the gap.

07Where the jobs are, and what the ads call them

The title is rare in local job ads and the work is common. SEEK's August 2026 New Zealand Employment Report found AI skills in 4.0% of all job ads, with mentions up 93.3% year on year6, and the Marketing and Communications category runs well ahead of that average. Employers describe the job with the tools and the outcome rather than the title.

Two Auckland examples from October 2026 show the pattern. Air New Zealand's Marketing Data and Technology Specialist ad asks for experience applying AI and automation, plus SQL7, and its Customer Lifecycle Marketing Specialist ad asks the hire to apply conversation design to AI and LLM-powered customer journeys8. Neither says marketing engineer. Both are hiring one. In Sydney, Lorikeet's GTM Engineer ad sat inside the marketing team and asked for building with Claude Code or Cursor9, which is as close as an Australian listing has come to naming the role.

New Zealand is slightly ahead of Australia on this measure. SEEK's July 2026 report put AI mentions at 3.7% of New Zealand ads against 2.1% in Australia10. The skills lag the use, though: Hays New Zealand reported in September 2026 that 60% of technology professionals regularly use AI tools and only 22% have had formal training or support11. If you are job hunting, search the tool names. If you are hiring, name the systems you want built in the ad, and the right people will recognise the job.

08When a New Zealand or Australian business needs one

The role earns its cost under three conditions: marketing that has to run every week whether or not anyone has time, an owner who will set the rules and approve changes in writing, and enough recurring work that building a system beats doing it by hand. Below that, a good marketer with simple tools is the better buy.

  1. Weekly marketing with nobody spare to do it. Content that should go out every week, enquiries that should be answered the same day, a search presence that should be maintained. If these slip whenever the business gets busy, a system is the answer and a marketing engineer builds it.
  2. One owner for the rules. Someone on the business side sets what the agents may touch and signs off the positioning, and answers in writing when a change falls outside the rules. If every decision needs a meeting, the system's speed advantage disappears into the calendar.
  3. Enough recurring work to pay for a build. A one-off campaign does not need an engineer. A month of content every month does. Gartner predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027 due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls12. Most of those will be projects that had no recurring job to pay them back.

For a 5 to 50 person business, the order that works is one system first, built on your own accounts with someone inside trained to review it, then a full-time seat once several systems run weekly and need constant tuning. Large paid media budgets and production-heavy brand campaigns still belong with an agency. The guide to what an AI marketing agent is explains what one of those systems looks like from the inside.

A marketing engineer builds the systems that produce a company's marketing, runs them on the company's own accounts and keeps the judgement over what ships. 11Questions owners ask

What does a marketing engineer do in New Zealand?

The same job as anywhere else, on a smaller market. A marketing engineer writes the company's positioning into a system, builds the agents that do the recurring marketing work on the company's own accounts, reviews every output before release, measures with sourced numbers, and documents the system so the team can run it. In New Zealand the work usually sits inside a 5 to 50 person business or a contract build.

Is there a marketing engineer job market in New Zealand or Australia yet?

Under that title, barely. SEEK New Zealand searches in October 2026 found no live ad with the exact title in the leading results, and Australian ads are few. The work is advertised under neighbouring titles: marketing data and technology specialist, lifecycle marketing specialist, growth manager and GTM engineer. Search for the tools named in the ad, such as SQL, HubSpot, Braze or Claude Code, rather than the title.

What does a marketing engineer earn in New Zealand and Australia?

There is no published band for the title in either country. The nearest advertised bands on SEEK New Zealand in October 2026 put a marketing manager at NZ$105,000 to NZ$125,000, and Talent International gives indicative estimates near NZ$195,000 for senior AI roles. Expect a marketing engineer to sit between those, depending on how much building the role carries.

How is a marketing engineer different from a marketing manager?

A marketing manager directs people and agencies to produce the marketing and reports on the result. A marketing engineer builds the system that produces it, so the recurring work runs without a team behind every piece, and then reviews what the system sends. Many small businesses need the second role before they can afford the first one properly.

Does a small business in NZ or Australia need a full-time marketing engineer?

Usually not at first. A business of 5 to 50 people gets more from one system built on its own accounts, with someone inside trained to review it, than from a full-time seat. A full-time hire pays off once several systems run every week and need constant tuning. A large paid media budget still belongs with an agency.

How do I tell a real marketing engineer from someone who has renamed their title?

Ask to see a system that is running now: what it reads, what it produces each week, who approves the output, what broke last month and how it was fixed. A real one can show you the files and the review record. A relabelled marketer can show you a deck.
