How does New Zealand's AI Advisory Pilot fund AI for small businesses?

The government will pay up to half the cost of expert help to plan and implement AI in a small business, until 30 June 2027 or until places run out. This guide covers what the pilot pays for, who qualifies, how to apply, and how it compares with the training fund that sits beside it.

The short answer

New Zealand's AI Advisory Pilot is a government grant, delivered through the Regional Business Partner Network, that pays up to 50% of eligible costs for expert AI help: up to $2,500 towards an AI roadmap and up to $12,500 towards implementation. Businesses need an NZBN and fewer than 50 full-time employees. It is open until 30 June 2027.

01What is the AI Advisory Pilot?

The AI Advisory Pilot is a New Zealand Government grant, delivered through the Regional Business Partner Network (RBPN), that co-funds expert help for small businesses to plan where AI can add value and then put it into practice. It runs in two stages, an AI roadmap and then implementation, and your business pays at least half of the cost.

The business.govt.nz Funding Explorer page for the AI Advisory Pilot (last reviewed 22 September 2026) describes two stages1:

  • AI roadmap (planning). Assess your business needs, identify opportunities to use AI and develop a plan for implementation.
  • Implementation. Put AI into your business processes and support your team to use it.

The pilot launched in January 2026. Human Resources Director reported on 19 May 2026 that Small Business and Manufacturing Minister Cameron Brewer had expanded the pilot from 50 businesses to 1502 and widened eligibility. For where AI fits in a small firm's marketing, start with the guide to AI marketing for NZ small business.

02How much does the AI Advisory Pilot pay?

The pilot pays up to 50% of eligible costs, with a ceiling of $2,500 towards an AI roadmap and $12,500 towards implementation support, so $15,000 at most across both stages. Your business pays at least the other 50%. Funding is limited and allocated competitively, so meeting the criteria does not guarantee you a place.

Worked through: a roadmap costing $5,000 could attract up to $2,500, and an implementation project costing $25,000 could attract up to $12,500. Above those amounts the grant stops at the cap and the business pays the rest. The official page does not say whether the caps include GST, so ask your growth advisor. To compare with unsubsidised prices, see the guide to AI marketing costs in Australia and New Zealand.

03Who is eligible for the AI Advisory Pilot?

An eligible business has a New Zealand Business Number (NZBN), fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees, and an established operation that is ready to explore practical uses of AI. Māori trusts and incorporations are included. The goal must be productivity and efficiency inside your own business, which describes most 5 to 49 person firms that are curious about AI.

The criteria on business.govt.nz, checked 8 October 2026:

  • Business type. A New Zealand business with an NZBN, including Māori trusts and incorporations.
  • Size. Small to medium, with fewer than 50 full-time equivalent (FTE) employees.
  • Stage. Established and ready to explore practical uses of AI.
  • Goal. Improving productivity and efficiency using AI.

The size test counts FTE, so a business with more than 50 people on the books, some part-time, may still fall under the line. The page does not define "established" in years of trading; your growth advisor makes that call.

04What the AI Advisory Pilot will not pay for

The pilot excludes AI products or services built to sell, general software or product development, training unrelated to implementing AI, work completed before funding is approved, and costs outside the agreed project scope. The last two catch owners most often: start early, or let the scope drift, and that spend is yours alone.

  • Products for sale. An AI feature inside a product your customers buy is out of scope. The pilot covers AI in how your business runs.
  • General development. A new app or website with an AI label on it is still software development.
  • Early work. Hold off on signing a contract or paying a deposit until approval is confirmed.
  • Scope creep. Anything added after the scope is agreed falls outside it. Raise changes with your growth advisor first.

05How do you apply for the AI Advisory Pilot, and when does it close?

You apply by registering your business with the Regional Business Partner Network and speaking with a growth advisor, who will confirm whether the pilot suits what you want to do. Business.govt.nz estimates four to 12 hours of effort. As at 8 October 2026, the official page says the pilot is open until 30 June 2027.

  1. Register with the RBPN using the link on the official AI Advisory Pilot page on business.govt.nz1.
  2. Talk to a growth advisor about the jobs you want AI to take on.
  3. If the pilot fits, complete the application and agree the project scope.
  4. Wait for funding approval before any work starts.

The closing date has moved, which is why older articles disagree. First planned as a six-month run, the pilot was extended in May 2026 to 31 January 2027. On 22 September 2026, Inside Government reported the Minister's announcement that the pilot would run until 30 June 2027 with a further 120 places3, and that around 150 businesses had joined since January. The business.govt.nz page now shows 30 June 2027 as the official date.

Because places are limited and allocated competitively, the practical deadline may arrive first. Confirm the current date and remaining capacity with your growth advisor.

06What is the Management Capability Development Fund?

The Management Capability Development Fund is a separate RBPN subsidy for management training. It pays up to 50% of one-to-one training and up to 75% of group training, capped at $5,000 per business each year, so the business pays at least 50% or 25% respectively. Marketing is an eligible subject, and AI appears as a group digital-tools subject.

The business.govt.nz page for the Management Capability Development Fund (last edited 3 August 2026) sets the cap at $5,000 per business each year4 and lists marketing, strategy and digital technology and tools among its training areas. The size rule matches the pilot: established New Zealand businesses with fewer than 50 FTE, including Māori trusts and incorporations managing Māori assets. Funding is available while it lasts, and only for approved training from registered service providers.

The detail sits on the RBPN eligible service subjects page (last reviewed 21 July 2025), which lists AI under group digital tools training5. For marketing and AI, two points matter:

  • Marketing is eligible in both formats. Group (one-to-some) training covers marketing fundamentals. One-to-one training covers marketing strategies, including "digital marketing strategy and social media planning".
  • AI appears under group training. Artificial intelligence sits in the one-to-some digital tools subject, next to CRM and booking systems. The one-to-one list does not name AI.

Exclusions on the same page that matter for marketing and AI work:

  • Consultancy that does the work for the owner. The fund pays for training and coaching owners to do it themselves.
  • Diagnostic and assessment services, including digital systems audits.
  • Website design, development and build, and implementation consulting.
  • Software-specific technical and product training, such as Microsoft Office, Xero or MYOB courses.
  • Services already subsidised through other government funding.

The RBPN's becoming a service provider page confirms the business contributes at least 50% for one-on-one training and at least 25% for group training6. Providers need a New Zealand base, an NZBN, Professional Indemnity insurance of $1 million or more and a track record of training small businesses. The network wants organisations that teach and upskill others, so hands-on implementation firms sit outside this pool.

Xero's research published in May 2026 found 47% of New Zealand small and medium businesses said practical training and workshops would help them feel more confident using AI7. Xero does not give a sample size on the page.

07AI Advisory Pilot vs Management Capability Development Fund: how do they compare?

The AI Advisory Pilot pays for expert help to plan and implement AI inside your business. The Management Capability Development Fund pays for training that builds your own management skills, including group AI courses and one-to-one digital marketing strategy. The first funds a project done with you; the second funds what you and your managers learn.

08How to prepare before you talk to a growth advisor

Arrive with your NZBN, your FTE count and two or three specific jobs you want AI to take on, each with the hours it takes now. A growth advisor can judge fit much faster from concrete tasks than from a general interest in AI, and the hours give you a baseline to measure the project against later.

  1. Your NZBN and legal business name.
  2. Your FTE number, worked out from hours.
  3. Two or three tasks where AI might help, such as answering enquiries or monthly reporting, with the weekly hours each takes.
  4. The tools you already pay for, since most AI work connects to your CRM, accounts or website.
  5. An owner inside the business who will review AI output after the provider leaves: your human in the loop.
  6. Your budget for your share, at least half of each stage, plus anything outside scope.
  7. Any work already under way. Work completed before approval is not funded.

If marketing is where you expect AI to help, the guide to building an AI marketing strategy for a small business sharpens the roadmap stage, and the guide to AI marketing transformation shows what implementation looks like.

09What to ask a provider before you sign

Ask every provider to confirm which programme they are approved for, the AI Advisory Pilot or the RBPN service provider pool, and to show you a roadmap or implementation they have delivered. Then ask who owns the systems, accounts and prompts once the project ends, and how scope changes are handled.

  • Are you an approved provider for the AI Advisory Pilot, or a registered RBPN service provider for the Management Capability Development Fund? Which one applies to this quote?
  • Can I see an example roadmap with client details removed?
  • Will the tools and automations run on accounts my business owns?
  • Who on my team will you train to run it, and how long does support last after handover?
  • How will we measure whether it worked, starting from the hours I gave you?
  • Are you independent of the software you recommend, or do you resell it or earn commission on it?

Some providers have announced their status publicly. The Icehouse announced on 22 January 2026 that it had been selected as an approved service provider for the RBPN AI Advisory Pilot8, working with Harnex on the technical side. Ask your growth advisor for the current list. The guide to AI agencies and AI consultants in NZ covers how advisers and builders differ.

11The short version

The AI Advisory Pilot co-funds up to half the cost of an AI roadmap (up to $2,500) and implementation (up to $12,500) for businesses with an NZBN and fewer than 50 FTE. Places are limited and competitive. Apply through an RBPN growth advisor before the official close of 30 June 2027, and confirm that date with them.

The Management Capability Development Fund is the training route: up to 50% of one-to-one and up to 75% of group training, capped at $5,000 a year, with AI available as a group subject. 12Questions owners ask

Can I use the AI Advisory Pilot and the Management Capability Development Fund together?

Possibly, for different things. The pilot funds expert help to plan and implement AI, while the Management Capability Development Fund subsidises management training. The RBPN's eligible subjects page excludes services already subsidised through other government funding, so the same service cannot be paid for twice. Whether you can use both in parallel depends on your situation, so ask your growth advisor before you commit to either.

When does the AI Advisory Pilot close?

As at 8 October 2026, business.govt.nz lists the AI Advisory Pilot as open until 30 June 2027. Earlier coverage gave 31 January 2027, which was the end date before the Minister's September 2026 extension. Funding is limited and allocated competitively, so places can run out before the official date. Confirm the current deadline and remaining capacity with an RBPN growth advisor.

How much does my business have to pay under the AI Advisory Pilot?

At least 50% of the eligible cost. The grant covers up to half, capped at $2,500 for the AI roadmap and $12,500 for implementation, so a business receiving the full $15,000 has spent at least $30,000 in eligible costs. Work outside the agreed scope, or completed before approval, is paid entirely by the business. Ask your growth advisor how GST is treated.

Is my business too big or too small for the AI Advisory Pilot?

The upper limit is fewer than 50 full-time equivalent employees. The business also needs a New Zealand Business Number and must be established and ready to explore practical uses of AI. The official page lists no minimum size, but it does not define established, so a very new business should check with a growth advisor. Measure the limit in FTE, which can sit below headcount when staff work part-time.

Does the Management Capability Development Fund pay for AI training?

Yes, as group training. The RBPN eligible subjects page lists artificial intelligence under digital tools for one-to-some (group) training, where the fund can cover up to 75% of the cost. AI is absent from the one-to-one list, which does include digital marketing strategy and social media planning. Software-specific product training, such as a course on one accounting package, is excluded.

Can the AI Advisory Pilot fund a new website or app with AI built in?

Usually it cannot. The pilot excludes general software or product development, and AI products or services built to sell to customers. It is designed to help a business adopt AI in its own processes and support its team to use it. A website or app project with AI features is likely to fall outside scope, though a growth advisor can confirm where a specific idea sits.
