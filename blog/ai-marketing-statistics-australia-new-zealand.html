The short answer

Around half of small and medium businesses in Australia and New Zealand now use AI in some form, but far fewer use it to run marketing work end to end. This page collects 62 figures on adoption, marketing use, AI search and time saved, each linked to its primary source and free to quote with attribution.

Last verified 10 September 2026. Every source below was opened and read before the figure was recorded.

AI marketing statistics are easy to find and hard to trust. Roundups quote roundups, American survey results get relabelled as global, and the same unsourced percentage travels for two years until nobody can say who measured it. For a business owner in Australia or New Zealand trying to decide whether AI belongs in their marketing, that is worse than no data.

This page collects the statistics that survive a check. Each one comes from the organisation that ran the survey or published the paper: the Australian Bureau of Statistics, the Reserve Bank of Australia, the Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman, MYOB, Xero, the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment, HubSpot, the Content Marketing Institute, ADMA, Salesforce, the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Gartner, Bain, Semrush and a peer-reviewed KDD paper.

Where a widely repeated number could not be confirmed on its original source, it is not here. That is the whole point of the page.

01How many small businesses use AI for marketing?

In Australia, 40% of small and medium businesses use AI, according to MYOB's April 2026 Business Monitor, while the Australian Bureau of Statistics recorded AI use at about 11% of small and micro businesses in 2024-25. Marketing is the most common use: Salesforce ranks marketing campaign optimisation first among SMB AI use cases.

The gap between 40% and 11% is not an error. The ABS asks a narrow question about the business as a whole in an official statistical survey; MYOB asks operators whether they use AI tools, which captures a marketer drafting a caption in ChatGPT. Both numbers are real, and a page that quotes only one of them is selling something.

02AI adoption statistics for Australia

Australian AI adoption is rising quickly from a very low base, and it rises with business size. Official ABS data puts overall business AI use at 12% in 2024-25, against 1% in 2021-22. Industry surveys of operators report higher rates because they count lighter, tool-level use of AI.

Read the GEO paper carefully, because it is the most misquoted source in this field. It reports that citations, quotations and statistics all raise visibility substantially, and that the gains are largest for pages ranked fifth rather than first, which is why a small business page can be lifted into an AI answer above a bigger competitor. It does not report a clean 41% for quotations and 33% for statistics, a pairing that circulates widely and does not appear in the paper. The AI search sprint is built on the findings that do.

07What the numbers mean for an owner

Adoption is no longer the question. On every measure that asks operators directly, most small businesses in Australia and New Zealand already use AI somewhere, and marketing is usually where it lands first. The real split is not users against non-users, but businesses that bolted AI onto old tasks and businesses that changed the work.

That split shows up as a performance gap. Ninety-five percent of B2B marketers work somewhere that uses AI applications, 89% use AI content tools, and only 39% say content performance improved. Australian marketers use AI weekly at 77% while 13% have had formal training. The tools arrived faster than the method, and the reported gains are concentrated in the minority who invested in the method: Xero's Trailblazers, 15% of New Zealand businesses, report productivity gains at 76% and time savings at 71%.

The second signal is where customers now ask their questions. If 60% of searches end without a click and consumers lean on AI-written answers for at least 40% of searches, then being quotable matters as much as ranking. A page with named sources, clear statistics and a direct answer under every heading is the format that gets lifted, which is what the KDD research measured and what this page is deliberately built as. The practical starting point is to find out which of your channels is actually broken, which is what the free AI-readiness benchmark is for.

10The short version

Australian and New Zealand small businesses have adopted AI marketing tools at 40% and 61% respectively on operator surveys, marketing campaign optimisation is the single most common use, and the measured benefit is hours rather than dollars. The gap that matters is method: 89% of B2B marketers use AI content tools and 39% report better content performance.

Before this shift, a small business competed for attention on budget and patience. After it, the deciding factor is whether the marketing runs every week and whether the business is quotable when a customer asks an AI engine instead of a search engine. Both are engineering problems, not spending problems.
