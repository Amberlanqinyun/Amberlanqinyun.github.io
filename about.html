Why Flow AI exists

I was the marketing team of one.

Flow AI exists because I needed it first.

Ten years in marketing, across every part of it. Positioning and category work. Search, back when that meant Google and now that it means ChatGPT too. Content calendars, brand voice, social, email, outbound, paid, events, analytics. Not one specialism: the whole function, in agencies and in house, for businesses from local operators to enterprise software.

Which means I know exactly what a marketer's week looks like. You are one person against a list that assumes five. The strategy sits in a document nobody opens because there is no time to execute it. The content calendar slips, then slips again. Leads arrive and nobody follows up for four days. You are not bad at your job. There is simply more work than hours, and every quarter the list gets longer.

Three years ago I started building my way out of it. Not prompts and not a tool subscription: real systems. Agents with the strategy written into them, content engines that hold a codified brand voice, outbound lanes that research before they send, search tooling that gets a business cited by AI assistants and not just ranked on Google.

Then I ran a whole marketing function on it. One marketer, no team, the full remit at an AI company: strategy, launches, content, thought leadership for three executives, events, and the reporting. The system did the production. I did the judgment. That is the only reason one person could hold it.

Flow AI is that system, opened up. Every business owner I meet has the same problem I had, with less time and no marketing background. So the agents that ran my function now run theirs.

How I work

How I work.

Four rules, applied to every system on this page.

Report, don't prescribe.

"I tested it, here's what happened" beats "you should." Observations from a real system, not advice from a podium.

Receipts persuade.

Numbers and screenshots, a before and an after. No receipt, no ship.

Failures are episodes.

What broke, the root cause, the fix, and what it cost. Published at the same rate as the wins.

Leverage over labor.

Anything done twice by hand becomes a system. The agent stack is the team; I stay on strategy and judgment.
